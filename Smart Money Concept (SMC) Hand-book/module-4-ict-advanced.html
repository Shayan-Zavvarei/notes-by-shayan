ICT Advanced Concepts

Time and inter-market context. When institutions deliver price, how the daily candle is built, where to enter with Fibonacci precision, and how the dollar and the clock confirm your bias.

Session times are real and stable (they shift ±1h with daylight saving). The deeper ICT constructs — killzone minutes, IPDA lookbacks, "the algorithm" — are interpretive models to test, not documented facts about any institution.

4.1

Sessions & Killzones — the liquidity relay

Asia builds the range, London sweeps it, New York delivers. Each killzone is a concentrated delivery window. Hover any session band to read its role and killzone window (UTC).

4.2

Power of 3 — accumulate, manipulate, distribute

The daily candle is built in three acts: the open accumulates, one wick manipulates (the judas swing, opposite the true direction), the body distributes. Play the day, then see the resulting candle.

4.3

Optimal Trade Entry — 0.618 / 0.705 / 0.79

After a BOS, anchor a Fibonacci swing-to-swing in the move's direction. The 0.618–0.79 band is the OTE; 0.705 is the sweet spot. It's strongest when it overlaps an OB or FVG. Toggle the confluence.

4.4

NWOG & NDOG — opening gaps as magnets

A weekend or daily opening gap is an un-traded range — pure inefficiency, like a large FVG. Its midpoint is the key reaction line; the full fill is a target. Play the return-to-fill.

4.5

Intermarket — DXY as the master key

EURUSD, GBPUSD and Gold are broadly inverse to the dollar index. Run your analysis on DXY and cross-check the pair. Aligned mirrors = high confidence; divergence = early reversal. Toggle the scenario.

4.6

IPDA — the draw on liquidity

A model of price being delivered toward liquidity and to rebalance inefficiency, referenced over 20/40/60-day lookbacks. The output that matters: where is price going next? Switch the lookback to see the draw shift.
